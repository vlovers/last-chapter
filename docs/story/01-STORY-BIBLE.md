# LAST CHAPTER — Episode 01: The Book Nobody Returned
**Narrative design draft v0.1 · Spoilers throughout · 3 in-game days · target 35–50 minutes (unvalidated)**

## Dramatic question
When you learn a private truth in a town where everyone knows everyone, are you helping by speaking — or taking away another person's right to choose when and how their story is told?

## Player role and boundaries
The protagonist has recently reopened the Last Chapter bookshop in Bellwick. Player chooses responses and book recommendations; the protagonist does **not** read minds, magically change memories, or become an omniscient detective. The player may politely finish transactions without personal questions. This costs optional depth, not basic progression. The game does not label privacy or disclosure universally good/bad.

## Town and opening
Bellwick is a fictional, walkable town with a bakery across from the bookshop, a flower shop near the bridge, a library in the old post office and a small railway platform. It has roughly 1,800 residents, but the playable episode uses a compact recurring cast. In October, the shop reopens after being shuttered for seven years. Previous owner Eleanor Vale has retired to a nearby coastal town; **she is alive, has not vanished, and is not secretly supernatural**. She left a box of uncollected book reservations. A note tucked inside says: *Some stories need more than one witness.*

The episode's central mystery is deliberately human: an old book contains a letter never delivered to its intended recipient. Two people have spent years interpreting the same silence in opposite ways. A third person may be harmed if the protagonist publicizes the letter without permission.

## Immutable truth — author-only
Seven years ago, **Iris Bell** (now 28) wrote a letter to **Leo Marsh** (now 29), confessing she loved him and asking him to meet her at the railway platform before she left for art school. She slipped the sealed letter inside a blue-covered novel, *The Winter Window*, which she entrusted to Eleanor to pass to Leo. Eleanor placed it on the reservation shelf; a plumbing leak forced the shop to close unexpectedly that night. The book was boxed up and never delivered. Leo thought Iris left without a goodbye. Iris thought Leo had received the letter and chosen not to come. Neither knew the book was lost. Eleanor had no malicious motive; her failure to follow up caused harm, and she is willing to acknowledge it.

Leo has been seeing **Mara Finch** (28) for nine months. They care about each other and have discussed moving in, but have not committed. Mara knows Iris and Leo used to be close; she does not know about the letter. Iris is not trying to sabotage their relationship. Iris returned to Bellwick because her mother is recovering from surgery and she is considering a local art workshop, not because she intends to win Leo back. There is no predetermined 'correct couple.'

The side story about **Anna** and **Thomas** is separate from the letter. Thomas has been meeting **Dr. Nora Reed**, a counselor at a community support group, on Wednesday evenings because he is worried about mounting debt and ashamed to tell Anna. **He is not having an affair in this episode's canonical timeline**. Mrs. Miller saw him outside the support center with Nora and drew an incorrect conclusion. This truth must not change based on player choices. Disclosure of an unverified rumor can cause serious damage; Anna and Thomas's eventual communication depends on their own decisions, not a bookshop owner's magical intervention.

**No parentage revelation in Episode 01.** Such a storyline requires deeper character groundwork and careful handling. Do not casually add a 'not your child' twist.

## Character and knowledge timeline
- Seven years ago: Iris writes letter, leaves novel; Eleanor boxes it during emergency closure. Leo and Iris misinterpret silence.
- Nine months ago: Mara and Leo start dating.
- Six weeks ago: Iris returns to town.
- Four weeks ago: Thomas begins Wednesday support meetings.
- Day 1, 09:00: shop reopens; unopened reservation box is in back room.
- Day 1, 11:20: Mrs. Miller mentions seeing Thomas and Nora; **this is an observation, not proof of romance**.
- Day 1, 14:10: Mara requests a book for Leo, who 'hates endings that feel unfinished.'
- Day 1, 19:00: player catalogs blue novel and finds an addressed but sealed letter. Player can note the name, not the content, unless choosing to open it.
- Day 2, 10:15: Iris asks for a book on returning home; a dialogue branch reveals she once left a letter here.
- Day 2, 13:30: Anna arrives and worries about Thomas; optional discussion can mention or withhold Miller's rumor, or recommend direct conversation without claims.
- Day 2, 17:10: Leo comes for a reserved book; may discuss his childhood friendship with Iris if trust permits.
- Day 2 evening: compare reservation card, dates, Iris's account, and the addressed letter; choose whether to contact Iris first, Leo first, Eleanor, or do nothing yet.
- Day 3, 09:30: consequences of Wednesday rumor spread surface, if player shared it.
- Day 3, 12:40: Eleanor phones after the player requests clarification; she explains the emergency closure and apologizes.
- Day 3, 16:00: player may return the sealed letter to Iris, offer it to Leo with Iris's permission, keep it secured for later, or (harmful but supported) disclose its existence to Mara without consent. The choice changes trust and future conversations; it does not rewrite the original letter.
- Day 3, 19:30: closing scene at the bookshop. Epilogue reflects known facts, relationships and boundaries, not a verdict on whether the player 'won.'

## Branches and endings (not morality scores)
**E1 — Returned with care:** Player asks Iris before involving Leo. Iris decides whether to send it herself. Leo may read it later; Mara is not automatically betrayed. End emphasizes agency and the cost of lost time.

**E2 — Uninvited revelation:** Player reveals the letter's existence to Leo or Mara before asking Iris. Information travels quickly. Iris feels exposed, and relationships strain. This is not a forced breakup; characters respond according to established trust and what was actually disclosed.

**E3 — Kept safe:** Player records the letter and holds it securely rather than intervening. The truth remains unresolved for now, but a new conversation becomes possible in a future chapter. The ending is bittersweet, not a 'bad ending.'

**Secondary Anna/Thomas resolution:** If rumor repeated publicly, Anna confronts Thomas while hurt and may stop visiting the shop. If the player instead encourages conversation or asks for evidence, they may disclose debt to each other privately. Their marriage cannot be 'fixed' by selecting a book.

## Reveal discipline
- Every revelation requires a prior clue, a credible speaker, or explicit consent.
- The player may find a sealed letter but must choose whether to violate its privacy; the UI should not silently read it.
- A scene may reveal a person's belief without confirming the underlying claim.
- The truth about Thomas and Nora never branches. What branches is who believes the rumor and how they respond.
- The player's silence is a real choice, with future cost and benefit, not an empty button.

## Tone
Cozy, witty, observant; avoid relentlessly tragic melodrama. Mrs. Miller's gossip is funny until it hurts someone. Books carry thematic echoes, not supernatural power. Use gestures, pauses and small actions instead of exposition dumps. Characters can refuse to answer, change their mind, or tell the player to mind their business.
