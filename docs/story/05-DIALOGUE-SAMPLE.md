# Episode 01 — Opening dialogue script (editorial draft)

**Scope:** Day 1 morning and evening. This is an authored sample, **not** the completed 40-minute script. All character knowledge follows the Story Bible.

## Scene 01 — The key turns (09:00)

*Art: October rain on Bellwick cobbles. One pixel-art bookstore background, shelves and counter on separate layers. A brass bell hangs above the door.*

**Narrator:** The key sticks on the second turn. For a moment, you wonder whether the shop has changed its mind about reopening.

**Narrator:** Then the lock gives way. The door opens with a little sigh, and the smell of paper and cedar meets you before the light does.

**Bookseller (thought):** Three hundred books inventoried. One temperamental kettle. And a town where I know approximately nobody.

*Across the street, someone in a flour-dusted apron waves from the bakery window.*

Optional response:
- **Warm:** “Morning, Bellwick. Be kind to me.” → Journal: *A beginning, however small.*
- **Practical:** “Right. Prices first, existential dread later.” → Journal: *A list helps.*
- **Quiet:** Straighten the crooked sign. → Journal: *Some things are easier to say with your hands.*

*The bell rings twice, although the visitor has only entered once.*

## Scene 02 — Mrs. Miller and Wednesday (11:20)

*Mrs. Miller wears a lilac coat, carries an empty jam jar and examines the shop as though it were a person who had been ill.*

**Miller:** “Oh. You've kept the old bell. Eleanor said it was cursed. Only by customers, mind you. It rings every time somebody wants a discount.”

**Bookseller:** “Should I be worried?”

**Miller:** “Depends. Do you give discounts?”

*She smiles without waiting for the answer and sets a folded list on the counter.*

**Miller:** “I want a mystery. A proper one. Clever enough that I can guess the ending before the detective. But if there's a corpse on page one, I shall complain.”

### Optional topics

**Ask about reading preferences**
- **Bookseller:** “Do you prefer solving the puzzle, or getting to know the people?”
- **Miller:** “Both. But if the detective spends forty pages describing his childhood, I'll solve the murder myself and send him the bill.”
- **Effect:** Recommend B04 (character mystery) or B13 (unreliable witnesses).

**Ask about Eleanor**
- **Bookseller:** “You knew the former owner?”
- **Miller:** “Everybody knew Eleanor. Knowing her and knowing what she thought were different occupations. The leak was dreadful. Three shelves of poetry ruined. Poetry! As if it hadn't suffered enough.”
- **Notebook:** Miller says the shop closed after a leak. Source = Miller; not yet corroborated.

**Ask about town life**
- **Bookseller:** “Has Bellwick changed much?”
- **Miller:** “The butcher became a florist. The florist became a yoga instructor. The yoga instructor ran off to Lisbon with the butcher. Not the same butcher, dear. We do have standards.”
- **Bookseller:** “And is everyone still speaking?”
- **Miller:** “That's harder to keep track of.”

**Stay professional**
- **Bookseller:** “Let's find your mystery.”
- **Miller:** “Businesslike. I approve. I was businesslike once. Nobody liked it.”
- **Effect:** Finish order without hearing gossip. No penalty.

### Gossip topic (unlocks after asking about town, or on a later visit)

**Miller:** “Speaking of people not speaking: did you see Thomas Hale on Wednesday? Near the old post office?”

**Bookseller:** “I don't know him.”

**Miller:** “Electrician. Tall. Walks like he's late to his own wedding. Lovely wife, Anna. Teaches at the school.”

**Ask what she actually saw**
- **Miller:** “He came out of the community center with a woman. Dark coat, short hair. They were talking as if they'd known each other forever.”
- **Notebook:** *Miller reports seeing Thomas with an unidentified woman. Firsthand to Miller; hearsay to player.*

**Ask what she thinks it means**
- **Miller:** “Well, dear, I think it's interesting. And I have lived long enough to know that interesting is rarely innocent.”
- **Notebook:** *Miller suspects an affair. Her interpretation, not established fact.*

**Challenge the conclusion**
- **Bookseller:** “That doesn't prove anything.”
- **Miller:** “No, of course not. I never said it did.”
- *She smooths her sleeve. For the first time, she looks a little less certain.*
- **Effect:** Respectful boundary; may unlock later correction.

**End conversation**
- **Miller:** “Now, about my detective.”
- **Effect:** No forced gossip interaction.

### Book recommendation

**B04 — The Orchard at Midnight**
- **Bookseller:** “Every suspect has a good reason to keep quiet. Nobody is hiding quite what you expect.”
- **Miller:** “A whole town hiding things? How imaginative. I'll take it.”
- **Later callback:** Miller recognizes her own rush to conclusions.

**B13 — Nobody Knows the Ending**
- **Bookseller:** “The witnesses disagree. The fun is finding the details that survive every version.”
- **Miller:** “Perfect. I shall keep notes. And if the author cheats, I shall write a letter.”
- **Later callback:** Miller compares town gossip to unreliable narrators.

**Other book**
- **Miller:** “Perhaps another day. Show me something with a proper mystery.”
- **Effect:** Retry without punishment.

*When she leaves, the bell gives a single clean ring. She waves her empty jam jar through the glass.*

**Bookseller (thought):** One book sold. One rumor acquired. Apparently, this is how the town keeps its accounts.

## Scene 03 — Mara's gift (14:10)

*Mara arrives with flour on her cuff and a bag smelling of warm cinnamon.*

**Mara:** “The bakery across the street. I waved this morning. You looked like you were negotiating with the door.”

**Bookseller:** “The door won the first round.”

**Mara:** “It usually does. I'm Mara. I brought something to celebrate your opening.”

*She slides over a lopsided bun.*

**Mara:** “Don't tell anyone I gave it away. My boss believes generosity is a contagious disease.”

**Bookseller:** “And what can I find you?”

**Mara:** “A gift. For someone who reads the ending twice. He likes trains and pretends not to like sentimental stories.”

**Ask about trains**
- **Mara:** “Schedules. Connections. The comforting fiction that a printed timetable means the world will behave.”
- **Unlock:** B11 *The Glass Station*.

**Ask what the gift should say**
- **Mara:** “That I notice him. Not that I'm trying to fix him. There's a difference, isn't there?”
- **Unlock:** B03 *Maps of Unfinished Roads* or B06 *Letters to a Stranger*.

**Ask his name**
- **Mara:** “Leo Marsh. If he comes in, don't tell him I called him sentimental. I would never hear the end of it.”
- **Notebook:** Leo is Mara's partner. Source = Mara.

**Keep it professional**
- **Bookseller:** “Let's look at the shelf.”
- **Mara:** “Thank you. I know I talk too much when I'm nervous.”
- **Effect:** Order remains fully solvable.

**Recommend B11**
- **Mara:** “He'll like this. And he'll tell me it was the historical detail. It won't be.”

**Recommend B03**
- **Mara:** “That feels like something we could both read. I'll take it.”

**Recommend B06**
- **Mara:** “A little on the nose. But maybe that's what gifts are for.”

*At the door, Mara turns around.*

**Mara:** “For what it's worth, I'm glad someone opened this place again. It makes the street feel less like it's holding its breath.”

## Scene 04 — The blue book (19:00)

*The shop is empty. Rain blurs the window. A cardboard box is marked RESERVATIONS, 7 YEARS AGO.*

**Narrator:** You have been meaning to open the box all day. It is smaller than you expected and heavier than it has any right to be.

Inspect in any order:
- **Receipt:** Reserved for LEO MARSH. Collection Friday, 17:00. No collection signature.
- **Book:** *The Winter Window*, blue cover, window illustration, creased spine.
- **Sealed envelope:** To Leo. From Iris Bell. Dated the same Friday. Seal unbroken.

**Record only facts**
- **Notebook:** Leo's book was reserved but not signed out. An unopened letter addressed to him is inside.
- **Effect:** Accurate deduction; evening mystery progresses.

**Assume he ignored the letter**
- **Notebook:** “Leo received a letter and never answered.”
- **Effect:** Mark as *unsupported assumption*, invite player to reconsider.

**Open envelope**
- **Effect:** Player violates privacy and gains a future Iris trust consequence. Exact letter prose remains to be written.

**Keep envelope sealed**
- **Effect:** Full investigation remains possible using exterior clues; no progress penalty.

**Narrator:** Outside, the last train sounds its horn beyond the rooftops. You look at the unclaimed book and wonder how many arguments in this town began with something nobody ever said.

**End of Day 1.**
