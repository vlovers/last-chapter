# Character Voice Bible — Last Chapter

**Purpose:** make the cast sound like different people in a real bookstore, not one polished narrator distributed across different names. This is an editorial production guide for runtime dialogue, not a claim that all recommendations are already implemented.

## The test

Hide speaker names and read a scene aloud. Can a reader identify who is speaking from rhythm, vocabulary, humor, what they notice, and what they avoid? If not, revise the voices before adding more lines.

Natural dialogue is not a string of perfect quotes. People interrupt themselves, answer the question they wish they had been asked, repeat a word when nervous, change the subject, tell a small story instead of giving an abstract answer, and sometimes say “не знаю”. Use these sparingly and character-specifically; do not add filler to every line.

## Voice fingerprints

### Mara — quick, warm, funny; hides exhaustion behind a joke

- Rhythm: short-to-medium phrases, quick pivots, questions back to the other person.
- Vocabulary: bakery shifts, dough, early mornings, customers, food; concrete rather than philosophical.
- Humor: affectionate exaggeration and small workplace absurdities.
- Under pressure: jokes first, then admits one plain, unpolished truth.
- Avoid: polished speeches about boundaries, care, or emotional growth. She can understand a feeling without naming it perfectly.
- Example: “Я сказала, що можу підмінити. А потім ще раз. Тепер начальник думає, що в мене взагалі немає дому.”

### Leo — precise and self-correcting; uses logistics to avoid vulnerability

- Rhythm: starts with a fact, adds a qualification, then catches himself overexplaining.
- Vocabulary: time, routes, platforms, missed connections, practical details.
- Humor: dry, slightly awkward, rarely performs for an audience.
- Under pressure: asks for dates or proof; when he becomes honest, his sentences get simpler.
- Avoid: making every thought a perfect train metaphor or an aphorism about uncertainty.
- Example: “Я пам’ятаю час. 18:40, здається. Ні, 18:45. Бачите? Оце я пам’ятаю. А що сказав — ні.”

### Iris — observant, exact, private; does not explain herself on demand

- Rhythm: quiet direct statements; notices a specific visual detail; can stop mid-answer.
- Vocabulary: light, color, edges, windows, paper, the physical act of drawing.
- Humor: dry and understated, often a small correction.
- Under pressure: becomes brief, not lyrical. She may say “Не хочу зараз про це”.
- Avoid: treating every line as an elegant metaphor or making her privacy stance a repeated slogan.
- Example: “Я перемалювала це вікно тричі. Не тому, що воно важливе. Просто вранці рама була синя, а ввечері — сіра.”

### Eleanor — practical, proud, sentimental in spite of herself

- Rhythm: begins with a practical complaint or correction, then lets one memory slip out.
- Vocabulary: shelves, keys, repairs, receipts, customers, the shop’s routines.
- Humor: dry, familiar, sometimes bossy.
- Under pressure: tidies objects, corrects dates, admits facts reluctantly; rarely narrates her own transformation.
- Avoid: having her state the theme of the entire game in a perfect closing line.
- Example: “Полиця там стояла двадцять років. И я знаю, что это не аргумент. Но попробуйте сами её передвинуть.”

### Mrs Miller — sociable, nosy, quick to connect dots; embarrassed by uncertainty

- Rhythm: starts a story before asking permission; adds details in parentheses; occasionally corrects herself.
- Vocabulary: neighbors, errands, who was where, old detective novels.
- Humor: gossip with comic confidence.
- Under pressure: fills silence with a theory; growth appears when she says what she actually saw and admits what she guessed.
- Avoid: turning her into a moral lecturer on evidence.
- Example: “Я не говорю, что он это сделал. Я говорю, что виделa его у двери. Хотя… нет, это был вторник? Надо было записать.”

### Nora — measured, careful, professional; warmer in the pauses

- Rhythm: asks one precise question at a time; leaves space rather than explaining every caveat.
- Vocabulary: what was said, what was recorded, what is known, what cannot be shared.
- Humor: subtle and infrequent.
- Under pressure: becomes more concise, not more bureaucratic.
- Avoid: sounding like a policy document or giving the player an ethics lecture.
- Example: “Я могу проверить дату. Кто приходил и зачем — это уже не моя история для пересказа.”

### Anna — thoughtful teacher who is less composed in her own life

- Rhythm: begins an explanation, catches herself, tries again more personally.
- Vocabulary: books, students, lessons, things she meant to do.
- Humor: gentle, often self-deprecating.
- Under pressure: asks what someone else needs before admitting what she needs.
- Avoid: perfectly balanced therapeutic reflections.
- Example: “Я детям всё время говорю: ошибаться нормально. А потом сама три дня переживаю из-за одной фразы на собрании.”

### Thomas — practical, quiet, shows care by doing; struggles to say what he feels

- Rhythm: plain statements, pauses, unfinished thoughts; prefers a task to a confession.
- Vocabulary: repairs, objects, food, work, what needs doing.
- Humor: understated, sometimes a one-word answer.
- Under pressure: offers to fix something; only later admits what the gesture meant.
- Avoid: eloquent speeches about remorse or emotional labor.
- Example: “Я починил защёлку. Она заедала. …Нет, я знаю, ты спрашивала не об этом.”

### The bookseller / player — curious but not omniscient

- Rhythm: short questions, occasional awkwardness, admits uncertainty.
- Role: the player can notice and choose; they should not diagnose everyone or always find the wisest response.
- Avoid: choices where every option sounds like the ideal therapist.
- Example choices should include a practical answer, an imperfect but sincere answer, and sometimes a respectful decision not to pry.

## Dialogue editing rules

1. **Replace abstract summaries with a small concrete detail.** Instead of “Я зрозуміла, що мені потрібні межі,” try “Я вже втретє погодилась на зміну. Завтра скажу, що не можу. Принаймні спробую.”
2. **Do not let every character finish with a quotable line.** End on an action, a joke, an unanswered question, a change of subject, or a silence.
3. **Let people disagree without instantly converging.** A character may say “Не думаю”, “Може бути”, “Я не це мала на увазі” or simply remain unconvinced.
4. **Use subtext.** If Mara talks about an extra shift, do not make her immediately explain the entire psychology of people-pleasing.
5. **Keep emotional insight uneven.** Characters learn over time; they should not all possess the same vocabulary for consent, boundaries, grief, and self-awareness.
6. **Keep choices human.** Avoid three versions of the same perfect response. Include practical, warm, clumsy, curious, and non-intrusive routes where they fit.
7. **Protect character agency.** The player may help, misread, or overstep; the other person is allowed to disagree, leave, or not forgive.
8. **Read aloud with speaker names hidden.** If voices cannot be distinguished, revise rhythm and attention before adding more text.
9. **Avoid fake messiness.** Do not scatter ellipses, stutters, slang, or typos everywhere. Each character has a different kind of hesitation.
10. **Do not make all dialogue more casual in the same way.** Distinction comes from what each person notices, avoids, jokes about, and can admit—not just sentence length.

## Editorial acceptance checklist for a scene

- [ ] At least one concrete want in the scene.
- [ ] A small friction, surprise, misunderstanding, or change of subject.
- [ ] The character's distinctive vocabulary or attention pattern appears naturally.
- [ ] At least one line that could not be reassigned to another character without rewriting it.
- [ ] No unnecessary summary of the theme.
- [ ] The character does not instantly agree just because the player chose a kind option.
- [ ] A fast route remains respectful; a deep route adds specificity, not a lecture.
- [ ] Consequences are carried by state and later scenes where the choice is meant to matter.

## Suggested workflow

Rewrite one representative scene per character first, then blind-read the dialogue with speaker labels removed. Do not rewrite every line at once: establish each voice, compare the cast side by side, and only then roll the style through the five days.
